About

Odobenii , the handpaint visualiser. It runs on this computer; photographs stay here. It is a way to see quickly how paint is distributed, not a quantitative tool: it shows where colour departs from a print's own tone, with no areas, counts or statistics, no claim to colorimetric accuracy, and never a claim that a place is unpainted.

Named for the walrus (Odobenus), a monochromat: Odobenii looks at monochrome prints and photographs with a paint layer on top.

When to box

Only places you have examined on the object and know are unpainted, at the tones where the boosted picture looks wrong: colour opposite to paint you can see, following one band of tone. Boxes that disagree with the others at the same tones are not used. Boxes correct the reference and set the stretch's statistics; they never show a place is unpainted.

The strip

A thin wash (about 1.3 ΔE, a faint one) and a small clear touch, set into patches of this print itself at each tone, where the reference holds: the capture's own noise, texture and blotches are in them. Drawn as the picture you are looking at draws. In each cell the right half carries the wash, and its dot the touch. A column is hatched where the print has no clean, even surface at that tone. Where you cannot see the wash here, a wash that thin would not show in the picture either.

The expected-unpainted picture

The print as the tool assumes it unpainted, its colour ×4. It should look like the print's own tone: colour that changes smoothly with lightness, with no forms or washes in it. If a face or a sky shows here in a colour of its own, the reference has taken in paint: box unpainted areas at those tones.

Some tones have too few places to judge: they are hatched in this picture, and the reference is guessing there.

What the pictures cannot show

  • Blank does not mean unpainted. A wash fainter than the strip shows, or matched to the print's tone, leaves nothing to see.
  • Neutral or tone-matched media never show: graphite, grey retouching, black spotting, white gouache, varnish.
  • Colour that is not paint shows like paint: foxing, stains, silver mirroring, uneven toning. Telling them apart is for you, at the object.
  • A broad wash over most of the print at some tone can enter the reference. Its sign: colour opposite to paint you can see, following one band of tone. Box unpainted areas at those tones.
  • Known issue: low-frequency noise shows as false colour. Broad, soft patches or gradients of colour in the boost and the stretch can be noise in the capture, not paint; a faint wash can look the same. Compare with the photograph and the object.

The boost and the stretches draw the print's own tone as neutral grey and exaggerate how colour departs from it. Their hue is the direction of departure from the print's own tone, not the paint's own colour. Odobenii is a way to see quickly how paint is distributed, not a quantitative tool: nothing here is a measurement, broad soft patches of colour can be noise, and blank does not mean unpainted.

Stop the server?

The visualiser stops and this page with it. The session is saved in its folder and reopens where you left off the next time the app starts.
